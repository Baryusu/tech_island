GOOD QUESTIONS. CLEAR ANSWERS.

Before we
begin.

Good design starts with a clear understanding of what your home can—and should—do.

Is this only for a new home?

No. We design for new builds, renovations and existing homes. Starting early allows better coordination with your architect and electrical team; a retrofit starts with an assessment of existing wiring, equipment and network coverage.

What actually works when the internet is down?

Compatible, commissioned local automations can continue through the LifeSmart hub while the hub and connected devices have power. Local wall controls remain available according to the selected design. Local app access needs a working local network and a supported, configured local login. Remote app access, cloud services and off-site notifications normally need an internet or available cellular connection.

What happens during a power outage?

An internet outage and a power outage are different. Mains-powered equipment needs a suitable backup supply to keep operating. We help plan which critical controls and network equipment should be backed up and coordinate this with the electrical and backup-power specialists. Runtime depends on the loads and the installed backup system.

Can you integrate the equipment I already own?

Often, yes—but compatibility is verified, not assumed. We review the exact models of your air conditioners, gate motors, lighting, pumps, audiovisual equipment, solar equipment and chargers before confirming an integration scope. Equipment warranties, safety controls and manual operation remain important.

Do you supply and install the solar panels?

Techisland focuses on smart-home design, energy monitoring and solar integration. Solar supply and installation are coordinated with your chosen solar specialist. We help align the smart-home scope with that provider’s equipment and design; solar is not presented as a Techisland panel-installation service.

Can EV charging be planned with the rest of the home?

Yes. We coordinate charger placement, power-capacity requirements, monitoring and compatible controls with your electrical and charging specialists. Charging capacity, solar-aware operation and any load-management features depend on the specific charger, vehicle, inverter and approved electrical design.

How much energy will a smart home save?

There is no responsible one-size-fits-all percentage. Results depend on your appliances, occupancy, comfort preferences, baseline usage and the completed design. We focus on reducing avoidable use and, where metering is included, comparing actual consumption rather than promising a fixed bill reduction.
